BIOGRAPHY: PRESIDENT ABRAHAM LINCOLN

Abraham Lincoln (February 12, 1809 - April 15, 1865) was the 16th President of the United States, serving from March 1861 until his assassination in April 1865. Lincoln led the United States through the American Civil War, its bloodiest war and its greatest moral, constitutional, and political crisis. In doing so, he preserved the Union, abolished slavery, strengthened the federal government, and modernized the U.S. economy.

Born in a log cabin in Kentucky, Lincoln was self-educated and became a country lawyer before entering politics. He served as a Whig Party leader, Illinois state legislator, and member of the U.S. House of Representatives before becoming the 16th President. His Gettysburg Address became an iconic symbol of American nationalism, and his Emancipation Proclamation paved the way for the abolition of slavery.

Lincoln's leadership during the Civil War and his efforts to preserve the Union while ending slavery have made him one of the most respected and admired figures in American history. His legacy continues to inspire generations with his commitment to equality, democracy, and the principles of freedom.
